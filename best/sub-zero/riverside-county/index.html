Sub-Zero and Wolf repair in Riverside County: independent shops that list them

10 shops in our Riverside County directory name Sub-Zero on their own websites — 4 based in the Coachella Valley and 6 serving Riverside, Corona, and the southwest county. 7 of them also name Wolf (one more shows a Wolf logo). Before you call any of them, check one thing: if your Sub-Zero or Wolf is still under the factory warranty, the warranty work has to go through factory certified service.

A brand name on a website is a claim, not proof of factory training or authorization. We list what each shop published, with the date we read it, and we do not rank them. Last checked: November 27, 2026.

Still under warranty? Start with Sub-Zero, not a repair shop

The Sub-Zero Residential Limited Warranty posted on the Sub-Zero, Wolf, and Cove website says:

  • Two years, parts and labor from the date of original installation, and five years, parts and labor, on the sealed system (compressor, condenser, evaporator, drier, and connecting tubing). Service under those warranties “must be performed by Sub-Zero factory certified service.”
  • Twelve years, sealed-system parts only. If you use non-certified service in that period, you have to contact Sub-Zero to get the repaired or replacement parts, and Sub-Zero says it will not reimburse parts bought from non-certified service or other sources.
  • To find certified service, the warranty points owners to Sub-Zero customer service (800-222-7820) or the company’s Certified Service Locator.

Wolf publishes its own warranty. The version for products with factory certified installation covers parts and labor for three years and certain parts (gas burners, heating elements, control boards, and others) for five years, again through Wolf factory certified service. Your coverage depends on your model, install date, and installer, so read the warranty card that came with your appliance.

Once the warranty is over, you can use any qualified shop. That is where the independents below come in. One of them, DeserTech in the Coachella Valley, says plainly on its homepage that it is not warranty-authorized for Sub-Zero or Wolf and works on them out of warranty — the kind of clear statement worth looking for.

Questions to ask before a tech opens a built-in

  1. Is my unit still under warranty? Find the install date. If it is within the Sub-Zero or Wolf coverage period, call the manufacturer or use the certified locator first.
  2. Are you factory certified, or independent? Either can be fine out of warranty — but if a shop says “factory certified” or “authorized,” check its name in the Certified Service Locator yourself.
  3. Have you worked on my series and model? Give the model and serial number from the tag. Older built-ins and newer ones use different parts.
  4. Where do the parts come from? Sub-Zero says its Factory Certified parts are genuine parts with a one-year replacement warranty (Sub-Zero, Wolf, and Cove factory certified programs). Ask whether a shop uses genuine parts and what warranty it gives on parts and labor.
  5. Is the service call different for a built-in? AV Appliance Repair, for example, publishes $85 for a built-in refrigerator visit versus $65 for a standard one. Compare published fees on our Riverside County service-call fees page.
  6. Will I get a written estimate first? California’s BHGS says service dealers must give you a written estimate, including the total price, before repairs (BHGS consumer guide). Ask for the shop’s BHGS registration and look it up.

Watch for brand-named websites

Search “Sub-Zero repair Palm Springs” and you will find sites with the brand name in the web address, a phone number, no street address, and a line in small print saying they are not affiliated with Sub-Zero. That pattern does not prove a site is bad, but it is not a factory service center either. Our checklist for spotting a lead-generation repair site covers what to look for. We have not listed sites like that here.
