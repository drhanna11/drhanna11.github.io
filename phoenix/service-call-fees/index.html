Appliance repair service-call fees in the Phoenix area: what local shops publish

Most Valley appliance techs charge a service-call or diagnostic fee to come out and find the problem. Here are the fees that independent shops in our Phoenix directory publish on their own websites — 5 shops with a dollar figure, from $55 to $125 — plus 7 more that explain their fee policy without a number, and what to ask before you book.

These are each shop’s own published numbers, copied with the date we read them. They are not our prices, not quotes, and not a market average. Fees change; confirm the amount for your address when you call. Last checked: November 24, 2026.

“Waived” vs. “applied toward the repair”

Almost every shop above uses one of two policies. They sound alike but can produce different bills, so ask which one you are getting:

  • Waived with repair (Robby’s, High Desert, Arizona Freeze, All City, Valley Services, Kaelric, Jazz): if you approve the repair, the visit fee disappears. If you decline, you pay only the fee.
  • Applied toward the repair (ProMaster AZ, AZ Major, Lux): the fee is credited to the final bill. The total can be the same as “waived” — or not, if the shop also has a minimum labor charge. Ask.
  • A range instead of one number (ProMaster AZ, $55–$125): the visit fee depends on the job. Ask for the figure for your appliance and ZIP code before the tech is dispatched.
  • Discounts have conditions: High Desert says discounts must be mentioned before payment is processed; Valley Services’ $25 off applies to online booking. Mention them up front.

What the published repair figures look like

Only two shops in our Phoenix directory publish repair-cost figures beyond the visit fee, and both are clearly labeled as typical, not quotes:

  • ProMaster AZ lists typical ranges of $150–$1,100 for refrigerator repair, $125–$600 for oven or stovetop repair, and $125–$300 for washer or dryer repair.
  • Valley Services says a typical repair is about $250, quoted after the diagnostic.

That is a small sample. Use it to sanity-check a quote, not as a price list — and if a repair quote is a large share of the cost of a new machine, read repair or replace? first.

Arizona: who to check, and the out-of-state phone number problem

Arizona does not have an appliance-repair registration like California’s BHGS that we could find. Some Valley shops also do HVAC or other contracting work and hold a license from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC), which you can look up in the ROC contractor search. The ROC’s “Before You Hire a Contractor” page has two warnings that apply to booking any home repair:

  • The local number may not be local. The ROC warns that the local phone number you think you are calling may be answered by a telemarketer in another state. Every shop on this page publishes its own number on its own website, and click-to-call on our profiles goes to that number.
  • Get the price in writing and do not pay in cash. The ROC advises written estimates, checks or payments made to the company named on the paperwork, and never letting payments get ahead of the work.

Source: Arizona Registrar of Contractors, Before You Hire a Contractor.

Questions to ask when you book

  1. What is the service-call or diagnostic fee to my address (give your ZIP code)?
  2. Is it waived, or credited toward the repair, if I go ahead? Is there a minimum labor charge?
  3. Is the fee different for built-in refrigerators, gas appliances, or a second appliance on the same visit?
  4. Will I get the repair price before any work starts?
  5. What warranty comes with the repair — parts, labor, or both, and for how long?
  6. Is the appliance still under the manufacturer’s warranty? If so, call the manufacturer first; an independent shop’s visit fee is usually not covered.

Have the brand, model number, and a short description of the problem ready; it helps the shop quote accurately and bring the right part.
