Appliance repair service-call fees in Riverside County: what local shops publish

Before an appliance tech drives to your house, most will charge a service-call or diagnostic fee. Here are the fees that independent shops in our Riverside County directory publish on their own websites — 7 shops with a dollar figure, from $25 to $129 — plus what “waived with repair” means and what California requires a shop to tell you first.

These are each shop’s own published numbers, copied with the date we read them. They are not our prices, not quotes, and not an average of the market. Fees change; confirm the amount for your address when you call. Last checked: November 23, 2026.

What “waived with repair” usually means

Most of the shops above use some version of the same policy: you pay the service-call or diagnostic fee only if you decide not to go ahead with the repair. If you approve the repair, the fee is either dropped or credited toward the bill. The wording matters, so read it closely:

  • “Waived” (AV Appliance Repair, NaSh, MMO, MG Service, Solvanta): the fee goes away when the repair is done.
  • “Applied to the repair” or “applied toward labor” (TechPulse, Tim’s Fridge Repair, Quick N’ Easy): the fee counts toward the final bill. Quick N’ Easy, for example, says labor starts at $175 and that figure already includes the service call.
  • Priced by distance (Appliance Showroom in Hemet): the same visit costs $76, $109, or $129 depending on which city you live in. Tim’s Fridge Repair says mileage may be added when travel is over an hour. If you live at the edge of a shop’s area, ask.

What California requires (BHGS)

Appliance repair dealers in California are regulated by the Bureau of Household Goods and Services (BHGS). Its consumer guide answers the fee question directly:

  • Trip charges must be quoted first. BHGS says a service dealer must quote a trip charge, if any, for each service call before it happens.
  • A reasonable diagnostic fee is allowed. The dealer can charge a reasonable fee to find the problem, and charges for the actual repair work only if you agree to the repairs.
  • Written estimate before repairs. Service dealers must give you a written estimate of the cost, including the total price, before repairs.

Source: BHGS, A Consumer Guide to Electronic and Appliance Repair. Law: Business and Professions Code section 9844 (PDF). To confirm a shop is registered, follow our step-by-step BHGS lookup guide.

Questions to ask when you book

  1. What is the service-call or diagnostic fee to my address (give your ZIP code)?
  2. Is it waived, or credited toward the repair, if I go ahead? Is there a minimum labor charge?
  3. Is the fee different for built-in refrigerators, gas appliances, or a second appliance on the same visit?
  4. Will I get a written estimate before any repair starts?
  5. What warranty comes with the repair, and does it cover parts, labor, or both?
  6. What is your BHGS registration number? (Then look it up yourself.)

Have the brand, model number, and a short description of the problem ready; several shops ask for it, and it helps them bring the right part.
